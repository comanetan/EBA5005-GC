How to use this tool

Viewing-Time Allocation

Use the Quick-estimate box: asking price, your fair-value guess, similar sales in the last year, one-way commute. Press the button and it computes the LP inputs for you.

Stretch-Budget Allocation

Market rates are pulled live from /api/meta (the project's own pricing model). Set your stretch budget, targets, and priority.

Final Recommender

Search real profiles and solve the actual 0-1 program the project runs in production. Choose Weighted-sum (default, trades goals off in dollars) or Preemptive (a strict ranking — a higher goal is never sacrificed for a lower one, no matter the $ saving). An optional chance-constraint alpha swaps the budget rule for a Monte-Carlo "P(price stays in budget) ≥ alpha" check instead of the default empirical band.

Trade-off Frontier

Instead of one shortlist, traces the whole curve of "how much more surplus would I get if I accepted $X more compromise" — answers "what would relaxing a requirement be worth" directly, without re-running anything.

Corner points

The best split of hours is always at a "corner" of what's allowed — one constraint or another is fully used up. The solver only has to check these points, not every possible split. The highlighted row is the winner.

Sensitivity — shadow price & reduced cost

Shadow price: if that row's constraint says "binding," it's fully used up — one more unit of it (one more liquidity-cap hour, one more agent slot) would be worth the $ amount shown. A "slack" constraint has room to spare, so one more unit of it is worth $0 — don't bother trying to loosen it. Reduced cost: shown only for a profile that got zero hours — it's how much that profile's hourly value would need to rise before any effort on it would pay off.

How to read this

The solver picked the k profiles (at most one per town) that together score highest on Net = Surplus − Deviation. Ranked best first.

  • Surplus — how much of a bargain this profile is: the market value of its attributes (location, size, age) minus what it actually costs. Higher is better; it's money the hedonic model says you're not paying for what you're getting.
  • Deviation — the $ cost of every way this pick falls short of what you asked for (mainly: farther from your MRT target than you wanted, priced at the market rate for that distance). Lower is better; $0 means it meets your stated goals exactly.
  • Net — Surplus minus Deviation. This is the actual number the solver maximises; it's why the shortlist is sorted this way.
  • Wait (mo) — expected months before a matching flat is likely to come up for sale, estimated from how often this exact profile (town + flat type + storey + MRT band) has sold in the past year. A low number means a liquid, fast-moving profile; a high number means a thin market where you may need to wait.

Solve summary

  • Solver status — "Optimal" means the solver proved this is the best possible shortlist given your constraints, not just a good guess. Anything else (e.g. "Infeasible") means no shortlist satisfies every constraint — usually the budget or lease floor is too tight.
  • Candidates considered — how many of the 712 real profiles were even eligible (within budget, meeting the lease floor) before picking the best k among them.
  • Objective achieved — the total Net (Surplus − Deviation) summed across every pick in the shortlist — the single number the solver was maximising.
  • Full k satisfied — "Yes" means a complete shortlist of k distinct-town picks was found; "No" means fewer towns had an eligible profile than you asked for picks, so the shortlist is shorter than k.

Methodology — ε-constraint multi-objective optimisation (Pareto frontier)

What would relaxing your requirements actually be worth?

The Final Recommender's "Weighted-sum" mode picks one point on this curve implicitly (wherever its $-for-$ trade-off happens to land). This traces the whole curve: at each allowed level of total goal compromise, the most surplus a shortlist could possibly capture.

What "Surplus" and "Deviation" actually add up

  • Surplus (bigger = more of a bargain) = shrunk value gap — the hedonic model's "market value of this profile's attributes minus what it actually sells for," pulled toward $0 for profiles with few recent sales so a thin profile can't fake a huge bargain — plus a preference tilt: extra $ value only for attributes (MRT, hawker, supermarket, clinic, park distance) where your stated weighting differs from how the market already prices them. Weighting something exactly like the market (multiplier 1.0, the default) contributes $0 tilt — you only gain or lose here by caring more or less than the market does.
  • Deviation (bigger = falls further short of your goals) = the sum of every dollar-priced shortfall vs. a goal you set: MRT distance beyond your target, missing walkable essentials (supermarket/hawker/clinic/park/school within their threshold), school-tier shortfall, storey below your target, expected wait beyond your target (priced at that town's real rent), and — if you set a minimum expressway distance on the Final Recommender tab — being closer than that. A pick that meets every goal exactly scores $0 deviation; there's no credit for exceeding a goal.
  • Each row below is clickable — it expands to show the exact profiles (real town/flat-type/storey/MRT-band combinations) behind that point on the curve, with this same $ breakdown per pick, not just the totals.
